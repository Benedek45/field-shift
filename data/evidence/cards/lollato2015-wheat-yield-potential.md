## Findings
- Under nonlimiting management in central Oklahoma, hard red winter wheat grain yield ranged from 3.06 to 7.68 Mg per ha, with the highest yield under irrigation in 2013-2014 and 7.11 Mg per ha at one dryland site in 2012-2013.
- Aboveground dry matter at maturity ranged from 9.95 to 20.5 Mg per ha, but harvest index did not exceed 0.41.
- Water use efficiency ranged from 7.8 to 12.6 kg of grain per ha per mm of water.
- Radiation use efficiency ranged from 0.8 to 1.9 g per MJ.
- The authors conclude that 7.68 Mg per ha is the empirical maximum attainable wheat yield under nonlimiting conditions in the southern Great Plains, well above the 6 to 8 Mg per ha usually reported as the regional maximum.
## Conditions and limits
- Two seasons of intensively managed small fields with full pest, weed and fertility control; farm fields will not reach these yields without that level of management.
- Central Oklahoma only; the southern Great Plains has a strong east-west rainfall gradient that changes the attainable yield.
- Harvest index below 0.41 means most of the biomass is straw, which matters for residue and for any straw market.
