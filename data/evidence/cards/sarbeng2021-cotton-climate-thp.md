## Findings
- About 60% of cotton acreage in the Texas High Plains is irrigated, and the region's cotton production depends mostly on irrigation to raise yield.
- Over 1987-2017 annual mean temperature had a smaller effect on cotton yield than the correlation analysis expected, and the correlation between annual precipitation and cotton yield was insignificant.
- High temperatures during boll opening reduced cotton yield, while precipitation during boll development and boll opening was needed.
- Mean temperature rose significantly over the three decades while precipitation trends were insignificant.
- The author notes that rising cotton yields may contribute to groundwater decline in the region, because more production means more pumping.
## Conditions and limits
- Correlation and trend analysis on aggregated regional data, not a field trial: it cannot separate irrigation, variety and management changes from climate.
- The study uses annual precipitation and temperature rather than crop-stage water balance, so the stage-level conclusions rest on monthly correlations.
- Thesis work, not peer-reviewed in the same way as a journal article; treat the numbers as regional context rather than as yield-response coefficients.
