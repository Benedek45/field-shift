## Findings
- Over 12 years in North Dakota, an intensive annual rotation of spring wheat, winter wheat and sunflower generated higher profits than spring wheat-fallow, but the profits were more variable.
- Within the intensive rotation, minimum tillage gave higher profits than conventional or no-till at the corresponding nitrogen rates, and minimum tillage profits were also more variable.
- The highest nitrogen rates tested gave the largest profits: 101 kg N per ha in the intensive rotation and 45 kg N per ha in wheat-fallow.
- Stochastic dominance analysis found spring wheat-fallow and the conventionally tilled intensive system economically inefficient compared with the intensively cropped minimum-till and no-till systems.
- Annual precipitation over the 12 years ranged from 206 to 655 mm and averaged 422 mm.
## Conditions and limits
- Northern Great Plains site with sunflower in the rotation; the economics depend on sunflower price and on the tillage machinery cost structure of the period.
- Prices and costs are historical; the ranking of systems is more transferable than the absolute profit levels.
- Dryland only, and the rotation relies on a broadleaf break crop that must have a market.
