## Findings
- Replacing fallow with a legume cover crop in a winter wheat-fallow system at Akron, Colorado reduced available soil water at wheat planting by 55 mm when the legume was terminated early and by 104 mm when terminated late, compared with conventionally tilled fallow.
- There were generally no significant differences in available soil water at wheat planting among the four legume species tested (Austrian winter pea, spring field pea, black lentil, hairy vetch).
- Average wheat yield was linearly related to available soil water at wheat planting, with the slope varying by year according to evaporative demand and April, May and June precipitation.
- The authors concluded that the water cost and the resulting wheat yield decrease may be too large to justify legume fallow cover crops in wheat-fallow in semiarid environments.
## Conditions and limits
- Semiarid dryland at Akron, Colorado, with about the lowest rainfall in the wheat belt; in a wetter environment the water penalty would be smaller.
- Legumes were planted in early April and terminated at two-week intervals from early June, so the water cost depends strongly on termination date.
- The comparison is against conventionally tilled fallow, not against no-till fallow, which stores water differently.
