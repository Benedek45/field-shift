## Findings
- Soybean nitrogen fertilizer replacement values reported in the literature range from 0 to 188 lb of N per acre for the first corn crop after soybean.
- The authors recommend applying a replacement value of 20 to 30 lb of N per acre for first-year corn after soybean, with minimal risk of reducing yield.
- Soybean yield was not a reliable predictor of the replacement value, so a high soybean yield does not justify a larger nitrogen credit.
- The credit should be applied only for one year after soybean.
- The review separates nitrogen rotation effects, which fertilizer can replace, from non-nitrogen rotation effects (pest cycle interruption, soil physical properties, moisture availability, mycorrhizal changes) that fertilizer cannot replace.
## Conditions and limits
- A literature synthesis, so the 0 to 188 lb per acre range reflects real site and year variation rather than measurement error.
- The recommended 20 to 30 lb per acre is a cautious minimum, not the average of the literature; fields with a history of manure or high organic matter may differ.
- The review calls for more work on how rotation, tillage, soil type and weather change the credit.
