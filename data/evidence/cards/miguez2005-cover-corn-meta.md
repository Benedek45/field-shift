## Findings
- Meta-analysis of thirty-six studies from the USA and Canada: biculture winter cover crops increased the following corn yield by 21%.
- Grass winter cover crops neither increased nor decreased corn yield overall, and that result did not depend on whether nitrogen fertilizer was applied.
- Legume winter cover crops increased corn yield by 37% when no nitrogen fertilizer was applied, and the benefit declined as nitrogen fertilizer rate rose.
- The response ratio compares corn after a cover crop with corn after no cover, so the legume gain is largest where the corn crop is nitrogen-limited.
## Conditions and limits
- The biculture result rests on a small number of studies, so the 21% figure has wide variation.
- Cover crops here are winter covers in temperate North America; the kill date, species and tillage vary across the pooled studies.
- The legume benefit is a nitrogen substitution effect: on a field already fertilised at a high rate it largely disappears.
## Quotes
- "Thirty‐six studies were included in the analysis representing different regions of the USA and Canada under different agricultural practices (i.e., species, fertilization, kill date, tillage, etc.)."
- "Biculture WCC increased corn yield by 21%, but there is greater variation due to the small number of studies in this group."
- "Legume WCC increased corn yield by 37% when no nitrogen (N) fertilizer was applied and this benefit decreased with application of N fertilizer."
