## Findings
- Barley grown after field pea derived 13% to 23% of its nitrogen (9 to 23 kg N per ha) from below-ground nitrogen of the previous crop, against 6% to 16% (3 to 9 kg N per ha) for canola following barley.
- Fertilizer nitrogen made up 8% to 11% of barley nitrogen uptake but 23% to 32% of canola nitrogen uptake.
- Straw nitrogen contributed only 1% to 3% of plant nitrogen uptake, so the residue itself is a small direct nitrogen source.
- No-till increased soil microbial biomass nitrogen, crop yield and nitrogen uptake compared with conventional tillage, probably through soil moisture conservation, without changing extractable soil inorganic nitrogen.
- Straw retention raised microbial biomass nitrogen in two of three years and lowered extractable inorganic nitrogen at seeding, but crop yield and nitrogen uptake at maturity did not differ between straw treatments.
## Conditions and limits
- Short-season Alberta system with barley, canola and field pea; the legume nitrogen contribution is from below-ground pea residues, not from a cover crop.
- Three years of data on one soil; below-ground nitrogen estimates from nitrogen-15 labelling carry methodological uncertainty.
- The rotation is a three-crop canola-barley-pea sequence, not a corn-soybean system.
