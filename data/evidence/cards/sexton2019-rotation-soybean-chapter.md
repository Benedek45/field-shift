## Findings
- Trials in South Dakota, Minnesota, Wisconsin and Nebraska show a 10% to 22% corn yield benefit when corn follows soybean rather than corn following corn.
- Soybean yields are about 8% to 10% greater in rotation with corn than in continuous soybean.
- The chapter describes a stacked rotation (corn, corn, soybean, soybean, wheat, wheat) as a way to reduce selection pressure on resistant pests by lengthening the interval between crops.
- A cited 15-year Wisconsin study found the corn-soybean rotation more profitable and less risky over time than continuous corn, and also more profitable than rotations including oats and alfalfa at that site.
- In the cited figure the plots received 163 kg per ha of nitrogen, so the corn-after-corn yield drag appears even at a full nitrogen rate.
## Conditions and limits
- Extension summary that reports other authors' trials rather than one experiment; the 10% to 22% range spans several states and years, and single farms will fall outside it.
- Upper Midwest rainfed conditions; in a hotter or drier region continuous corn performs differently and the rotation benefit changes.
- Profitability of longer rotations depends on markets for the small grain and forage, which the chapter notes explicitly.
