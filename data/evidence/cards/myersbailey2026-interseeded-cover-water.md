## Findings
- A 3-year comparison of Kentucky bluegrass perennial groundcover, two interseeded annual cover crop systems and a no-cover control found treatment effects were clearest in the wettest year, when rain after a dry season produced a summer nitrate flush.
- Averaged over the study, the high-biomass wide-row interseeded cover crop reduced flow-weighted nitrate concentration in subsurface drainage by 22% but reduced maize grain yield by 20%.
- The Kentucky bluegrass perennial groundcover reduced nitrate concentration by 20% with no associated grain yield loss.
- All cover crop systems reduced nitrate concentrations during the high-leaching summer period compared with the no-cover control.
- The authors highlight the yield trade-off of wide-row annual cover cropping and present perennial groundcover as a promising way to cut early summer nitrate loss.
## Conditions and limits
- Three years with below-average precipitation, so the leaching signal is concentrated in one wet year and the averages span very different seasons.
- Small plots with three replicates and weekly drainage monitoring; the water-quality result is for subsurface drainage, not surface runoff.
- Kentucky bluegrass as a living mulch is a novel system with its own management problems (competition, termination), so the no-yield-loss result may not hold in other environments.
