## Findings
- The optimum nitrogen rate for corn grown after soybean can be lowered by up to 30 lb N per acre compared with corn following corn.
- After an over-wintering cover crop such as cereal rye or triticale, the corn nitrogen rate can be lowered by 20 to 30 lb N per acre compared with corn with no cover crop.
- A cover crop planted after small grain harvest in July or August earns a credit of 40 to 50 lb N per acre for the following corn.
- The corn nitrogen rate after interseeded legumes can be lowered by up to 70 to 120 lb N per acre.
- Nitrogen uptake efficiency used in the Cornell guidelines ranges from 50% to 75% of applied and credited nitrogen, depending on soil and drainage.
## Conditions and limits
- New York guidelines built on the Cornell yield and soils database; the credits are book values for that state's soils and would need local calibration elsewhere.
- The credits are reductions to a recommended nitrogen rate, not a guarantee that the nitrogen is present when the crop needs it; timing and weather still matter.
- A winter-killed or thin cover crop does not earn the full credit, and the fact sheet points to a separate document for the exact rules.
