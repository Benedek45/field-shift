## Findings
- In a drought manipulation experiment using rainout shelters inside a 36-year rotation diversity and no-till trial, diversifying maize-soybean rotations with small grain cereals and cover crops reduced maize yield losses to drought by 17.1% plus or minus 6.1%.
- No-till did not affect maize drought resistance in the same experiment.
- Diversified rotations mitigated maize water stress at both leaf and canopy scale.
- Soil organic matter correlated strongly with lower maize water stress, although soil organic matter did not differ significantly between the rotations or tillage treatments.
- The soil organic matter effect was not explained by greater soil water retention, greater infiltration, or deeper root water uptake measured with stable isotopes, so the authors point to other mechanisms.
## Conditions and limits
- Temperate rainfed site with an artificial drought imposed by rainout shelters; the 17.1% figure is the change in yield loss under that imposed drought, not an average yield gain.
- The diverse rotations included small grain cereals and cover crops, so the effect is not attributable to any one added crop.
- One site and one soil; the link between soil organic matter and water stress is correlational within this trial.
## Quotes
- "We show that diversifying maize-soybean rotations with small grain cereals and cover crops mitigated maize water stress at the leaf and canopy scales and reduced yield losses to drought by 17.1 ± 6.1%, while no-till did not affect maize drought resistance."
- "Path analysis showed a strong correlation between soil organic matter and lower maize water stress despite no significant differences in soil organic matter between rotations or tillage treatments."
- "Crop rotation diversification is an underappreciated drought management tool to adapt crop production to climate change through managing for soil organic matter."
