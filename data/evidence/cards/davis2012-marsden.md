## Findings
- In the 2-year corn-soybean system maize grain yield averaged 12.3 Mg per ha over 2003 to 2011, against 12.7 Mg per ha in the 3-year and 12.9 Mg per ha in the 4-year rotation, a 4% gain for the longer rotations.
- Soybean grain yield averaged 3.4 Mg per ha in the 2-year system against 3.8 Mg per ha in both longer rotations, a 9% gain.
- Total harvested crop mass (grain, straw and hay) was 8% greater in the 3-year and 4-year rotations, averaging 7.9 Mg per ha in the 2-year system and 8.5 and 8.6 Mg per ha in the longer ones.
- The 3-year and 4-year rotations received composted cattle manure at a mean dry matter rate of 8.3 Mg per ha before maize, plus legume nitrogen from red clover and alfalfa, and used banded herbicides with inter-row cultivation in maize and soybean.
- Weed management in the 3-year and 4-year rotations used no herbicides in the small grain and forage legume crops, so the yield comparison is against a system with far lower herbicide input.
## Conditions and limits
- One Iowa site, rainfed, with a manure and legume nitrogen source in the diverse rotations: the yield gain is not attributable to rotation alone, since fertility and weed management differ between systems.
- The rotations include a forage legume (alfalfa) and a small grain, which need a market or on-farm use; the result does not say that a corn-soybean-wheat rotation without forage would match it.
- Yields were statistically similar between the 3-year and 4-year systems, so the fourth year is not required for the yield gain at this site.
