## Findings
- Over 15 years of a drought-heavy period, aboveground cover crop biomass totalled 22.8 Mg per ha with 209 cm of precipitation and 20 cm of supplemental irrigation.
- Cover crop biomass ranged from 0.39 Mg per ha in the low-precipitation winter of 2006 to 2007 up to 9.34 Mg per ha in the winter of 2000 to 2001.
- Fallow plots gained 4.8 cm and 4.3 cm of soil water in the top 90 cm over January to March in 2013 and 2014, while cover-cropped plots stored no additional water.
- Compared with fallow, cover crops depleted 5.3 cm of water from the 0-90 cm profile in 2013 and 0.67 cm in 2014.
## Conditions and limits
- Semiarid Central Valley with winter-rainfed cover crops and limited supplemental irrigation; in a wet winter the water cost is small, in a dry winter it is large.
- Water is measured to 90 cm only, and the study site is one long-term field, so the numbers describe this system rather than all California rotations.
- The authors conclude vigorous cover crop growth may not be possible in all years, and that biomass production may cost soil water in this drought-prone region.
