## Findings
- In a one-year Missouri study, winter barley grain yield was unaffected by whether the previous crop was soybean or sunn hemp.
- Barley yield was also unaffected by fall nitrogen rate at 22.4 or 44.8 kg N per ha, suggesting either legume left adequate residual nitrogen.
- Malting quality of the grain samples was adequate for the craft brewing industry.
- The authors conclude that the previous legume crop affected barley yield more than the fall nitrogen fertilizer rate did.
## Conditions and limits
- A preliminary one-year study, so the result is a first indication and not a rotation recommendation.
- Small nitrogen rates (22.4 and 44.8 kg N per ha) were compared; a zero-nitrogen control and higher rates were not part of the comparison described in the abstract.
- Missouri only, and the preceding crops were soybean and sunn hemp, not a grass cover crop.
## Quotes
- "Barley grain yield was unaffected by the previous legume crop choice or the fall N fertilization rate, suggesting that either crop provided adequate residual N to the succeeding barley crop."
- "This study's results reveal preliminary data supporting winter barley production in the lower Midwestern United States, with an indication that previously grown legume crops impact crop yield greater than fall N fertilizer rate."
