## Findings
- Non-host crops in rotation reduced soybean cyst nematode egg density from 4000 eggs per 100 cm3 in continuously cropped susceptible soybean to 1000 eggs per 100 cm3 in a 3-year rotation and 500 eggs per 100 cm3 in a 4-year rotation.
- Nematode numbers climbed back above 5000 eggs per 100 cm3 by the end of the season whenever susceptible soybean was grown.
- The susceptible cultivar produced 33% less grain and fewer pods than the resistant cultivar Manokin at the infested location, while the two yielded similarly at the non-infested site.
- Grain yields of the susceptible cultivar were similar in all rotations at each location, so rotation lowered nematode numbers without preventing yield loss in the susceptible crop that followed.
- Full-season and double-cropped soybean reacted similarly to soybean cyst nematode.
## Conditions and limits
- Rotation reduces nematode population density but the population recovers within one susceptible soybean season, so rotation alone does not protect a susceptible cultivar.
- Results are from a newly infested western US production region; population dynamics differ where the nematode has been established for decades.
- Cultivar resistance is the effective lever in this study; rotation is a supporting practice, not a substitute.
## Quotes
- "Nonhost crops reduced nematode densities from 4000 eggs 100 cm −3 in continuously cropped SCN‐susceptible plots to 1000 and 500 eggs 100 cm −3 in 3‐ and 4‐yr rotations, respectively."
- "However, nematode numbers rapidly increased to more than 5000 eggs 100 cm −3 by seasons end when susceptible soybean was grown."
- "Crop rotation reduced cyst nematode numbers, but this benefit did not reduce yield losses on subsequent SCN‐susceptible soybean as numbers rapidly climbed to damaging levels."
