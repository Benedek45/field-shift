## Findings
- Satellite-derived estimates for Indiana, Iowa and Illinois over 2007-2012 showed rotation yield benefits of 1.0% (range -8.9% to 9.7%) for corn and 10.8% (range 4.6% to 17.6%) for soybean.
- These satellite estimates were not significantly different from field-level estimates of 4.3% for rainfed corn and 10.3% for rainfed soybean from the same region and years.
- The satellite corn estimate (1.0%) is lower than the field-level corn estimate (4.3%), which the authors still treat as within the range found in the literature.
- The authors conclude that satellite data can be used to evaluate rotation practices without ground data, after adjusting for environmental differences between fields with and without rotations.
## Conditions and limits
- The satellite corn benefit is small and its range crosses zero, so the corn result is weak compared with the soybean result.
- Three Corn Belt states and six years only; the method needs a yield product at field scale, which limits where it transfers.
- Satellite estimates are compared with the field-level numbers, so this paper validates the method rather than providing independent new evidence.
## Quotes
