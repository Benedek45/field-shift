## Findings
- Field-level satellite analysis of more than 90,000 fields in the US Corn Belt in 2019-2020 found an average maize yield loss of 5.5% on fields that had used cover crops for 3 or more years, compared with fields that did not adopt cover cropping.
- Soybean yield losses on cover-cropped fields averaged 3.5%.
- Maize losses were larger on fields with better soil ratings, cooler mid-season temperatures and lower spring rainfall; soybean losses were larger with warmer June temperatures and lower spring and late-season rainfall.
- Because cover crop species and timing were not observed, the estimates describe cover cropping as currently practiced in aggregate, not the effect of any particular species.
## Conditions and limits
- Observational field-level comparison, not a randomised trial: unobserved differences between adopters and non-adopters can bias the estimate even with causal forests.
- The analysis covers 2019-2020 only and cannot separate species, seeding date or termination date, all of which experimental work shows matter.
- Losses are averages across fields; individual farms with well-managed cover crops can be above or below them.
## Quotes
- "Using causal forests analysis, we estimate an average maize yield loss of 5.5% on fields where cover crops were used for 3 or more years, compared with fields that did not adopt cover cropping."
- "For soybeans, average yield losses were 3.5%, with larger impacts on fields with warmer June temperatures, lower spring and late-season rainfall, and, to a lesser extent, better soils."
