## Findings
- A survey of 46 farmers in Georgia, Alabama and Florida in early 2021 found that most adopters ran medium to large operations and had integrated cover crops into their systems.
- The main barriers farmers reported were the extra time and labour, high seed cost and the complexity of cover crop management.
- The main benefits farmers reported were reduced soil erosion, better soil aeration and water infiltration, and increased biodiversity.
- Cover crop adoption produced cost savings where the cover was grazed, harvested for forage or harvested for seed.
- Participation in cost-share programmes remained low because of programme limitations and lack of awareness, and farmers asked for longer cost-share periods.
## Conditions and limits
- A survey of 46 farmers, so percentages describe this sample rather than the region; the abstract reports no monetary values.
- Self-reported benefits and barriers, which reflect perception as much as measurement.
- Southeastern humid row-crop systems; the economics differ where cover crop establishment is harder or where forage markets are absent.
