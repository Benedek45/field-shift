## Findings
- Dryland cereal rye on the Southern High Plains produced more than 1000 kg per ha of forage biomass even in a dry year, and light irrigation was needed to reach more than 1200 kg per ha for winter grazing.
- No-till management and timely termination of the winter cover crops were the key practices for limiting the negative effect of the cover crop on the following summer teff crop.
- No soil water detriment was attributable to planting no-till cover crops compared with the conventional practice of winter fallow.
- The authors conclude that producers can use soil-conserving winter forage cover crops in this region without significant soil water depletion.
## Conditions and limits
- Small-plot experiment on the drought-prone Southern High Plains, where the main concern is that a cover crop steals water from the summer crop.
- The summer crop here is teff hay, not cotton; water use of a full-season cotton crop after a cover crop is not measured.
- The no-detriment result depends on no-till and early termination; a tilled or late-terminated cover crop would dry the profile.
## Quotes
- "No-till management and timely termination of the winter cover crops were crucial to reducing the negative impact of winter crops on summer teff production."
- "The results indicated no detriment to soil water content that was attributable to planting no-till cover crops compared with the conventional practice of winter fallow."
- "Therefore, producers could take advantage of the soil-conserving attributes of high-quality winter forage cover crops without experiencing significant soil water depletion."
