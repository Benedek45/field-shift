## Findings
- In a dryland winter wheat-sorghum/corn-fallow rotation at North Platte, Nebraska, long-term winter wheat yield (1966-1983) averaged 2718 kg per ha under no-till against 2421 kg per ha under conventional till.
- Grain sorghum yield (1964-1988) averaged 4125 kg per ha under no-till against 3062 kg per ha under conventional till.
- The higher no-till yields were attributed to greater soil water retention from increased residue cover.
- Continuous no-till acidified the 0-15 cm layer at 1.3 kmol hydrogen per ha per year from 1962 to 2003, which is 38% of the total potential acidity from nitrogen fertilizer applications over 41 years.
- After 14 years of no-till following conventional tillage there were no differences in soil chemical properties compared with continuous no-till, so the chemical changes are slow and reversible only over decades.
## Conditions and limits
- One semiarid Nebraska silt loam site; the yield advantage of no-till comes from water storage and would shrink in a wetter climate or on a poorly drained soil.
- The wheat and sorghum yield periods are different decades, so the comparison mixes tillage with weather and with changes in varieties and management.
- The acidification rate implies that lime may eventually be needed under long-term no-till with ammonium-based nitrogen.
