## Findings
- In five site-years in Arkansas, cereal rye terminated two weeks before corn planting gave corn yield similar to the conventional system with no cover crop.
- Cereal rye terminated at or after corn planting reduced corn yield by 16% to 22%.
- Palmer amaranth control at the V4 corn stage was better than 75% in cereal rye environments regardless of termination timing, against less than 50% in the conventional corn system.
- Corn height was 8% to 10% greater where cereal rye was terminated one or two weeks after corn planting than in the conventional system or other rye timings.
- Yellow nutsedge control was slightly worse in cereal rye environments than in the conventional system.
## Conditions and limits
- Five site-years in Arkansas with a two-pass herbicide programme, so the weed result combines rye with herbicides rather than testing rye alone.
- The yield loss from late termination is a competition effect; a rye cover that is thin or that winter-kills would behave differently.
- Humid subtropical environment; the same termination window may not fit a shorter growing season further north.
