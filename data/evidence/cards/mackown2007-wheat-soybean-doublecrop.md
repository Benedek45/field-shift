## Findings
- Double-cropped soybean after winter wheat produced 1.35 to 1.90 Mg per ha of biomass when grazed or cut at seed fill, with crude protein of 129 to 220 g per kg.
- Three-year average nitrogen uptake by the double-crop soybean was 44 kg per ha.
- Wheat forage yield increased 29% with 112 kg N per ha, and crude protein was inversely related to yield.
- Double-cropped soybean gave no yield-enhancing nitrogen benefit to the following wheat and did not raise soil nitrogen or carbon after three years, even when used as a green manure.
- The authors conclude the dryland wheat-soybean double-crop forage system is of limited feasibility unless a producer accepts the low productivity of the soybean crop.
## Conditions and limits
- Dryland southern Great Plains, where soybean planted after wheat grain harvest runs into dry, hot conditions, which is the main limit.
- The soybean was managed as forage (grazed, hay, green manure, mulch), not as a grain crop, so the results do not describe soybean grain after wheat.
- Three years is short for detecting soil carbon change.
## Quotes
- "Soybean biomass ranged from 1.35 to 1.90 Mg ha −1 when soybean grazing and harvest occurred at seed fill, and crude protein ranged from 129 to 220 g kg −1 resulting in a 3‐yr average N uptake of 44 kg ha −1"
