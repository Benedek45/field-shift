## Findings
- Take-all of wheat was severe in all rotations that grew wheat every year, in a long-term study of 12 double-cropping sequences started in 1994 in the southeastern United States.
- A one-year rotation with canola significantly reduced take-all incidence and severity in the following wheat crop.
- In rotations where wheat followed one year of canola, wheat grain yield at the end of the second and third seasons equalled that of control plots with little or no take-all.
- Two consecutive years of canola did not suppress take-all or raise wheat yield any more than a single year of canola between wheat crops.
- Soybean or pearl millet as the summer crop had little effect on yield loss from take-all in a following wheat crop.
## Conditions and limits
- Disease-based result from inoculated plots in the humid Southeast; take-all pressure and canola performance differ in drier wheat regions.
- Canola needs a market and a suitable sowing window; the rotation benefit is specific to breaking the take-all cycle, not a general yield boost.
- Continuous wheat was the susceptible baseline, so the comparison shows the cost of wheat monoculture rather than an optimal rotation.
