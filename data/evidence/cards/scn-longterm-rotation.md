## Findings
- In a long-term corn-soybean rotation site, soil fungal community structure varied significantly with crop sequence, and five consecutive years of monoculture shifted communities toward those of the long-term monoculture plots of the same crop.
- Soybean cyst nematode density was positively correlated with the abundance and diversity of nematode-trapping fungi and with several potential nematode egg parasites.
- More pathotrophs were found under soybean and more saprotrophs and symbiotrophs under corn, which the authors offer as one explanation for the yield benefit of rotation.
- Soil phosphorus varied significantly with crop sequence and correlated with the relative abundance of several mycorrhizal and mortierellalean fungal groups.
## Conditions and limits
- This is a microbial-community study, not a yield trial: the abstract gives no soybean cyst nematode population numbers or yield losses.
- One site, one soil series and a single sampling campaign; correlations between fungi and nematodes do not establish cause.
- A preprint, so the results have not completed peer review at the version read here.
## Quotes
- "In this study, a long-term rotation study site with corn and soybean planted in annual rotation, five-year rotation, and long-term monoculture was utilized to examine the relationships between crop sequences, soil fungal communities, soybean cyst nematode (SCN, Heterodera glycines ) densities, soil properties, and crop yields."
- "These results suggest several possible explanations for the improved yields associated with crop rotation, including decreased pathogen pressure, modification of soil properties, and increased diversity of soil fungal communities."
