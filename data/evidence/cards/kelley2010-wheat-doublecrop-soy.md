## Findings
- Over 1996-2006 in southeastern Kansas, hard red winter wheat yielded more after corn or soybean than after grain sorghum in a 2-year rotation.
- Double-crop soybean yield averaged 20% greater when the preceding wheat crop followed corn or grain sorghum than when wheat followed full-season soybean.
- Tillage affected double-crop soybean yield more than wheat yield, and double-crop soybean often yielded more under continuous no-till than under conventional tillage or a single no-till pass per cycle.
- At the end of the study total carbon and total nitrogen were greater under no-till in the top 3 inches but greater under conventional tillage at 3 to 6 inches.
## Conditions and limits
- Eastern Great Plains multi-cropping system with wheat, soybean, corn and sorghum; results depend on the double-crop window after wheat harvest.
- One Kansas site over ten years; the rotation effects are averages and the paper does not separate weather years.
- The soil carbon result is a depth redistribution, not a whole-profile gain, so no-till does not simply add carbon at this site.
