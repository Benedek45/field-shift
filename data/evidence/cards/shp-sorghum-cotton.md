## Findings
- Continuous cotton had 41% higher weed density and 57% greater early-season weed biomass than the sorghum-cotton rotation in a two-year field experiment at Lubbock, Texas.
- Cotton plants in the sorghum-cotton rotation were 17% to 22% taller and had 36% to 90% more biomass than cotton in monoculture.
- With weed control applied, cotton lint yield in the sorghum-cotton rotation was 53% higher and seed yield 30% higher than in continuous cotton.
- Without weed control, lint and seed yields were still 21% to 41% greater in the sorghum-cotton system because of lower weed density and biomass.
- More than 90% of cotton acreage in the Texas High Plains relies on herbicides, which the authors give as the reason herbicide-resistant weeds drive interest in rotation.
## Conditions and limits
- Two seasons only (2024-2025) on one research farm, so the weed-suppression benefit is measured over a single rotation cycle.
- Sorghum has to fit the farm's market and its own herbicide and rotation constraints; the paper tests one sorghum-cotton sequence, not longer rotations.
- Weed density differences are partly a herbicide-programme effect, since the cropping systems were compared with and without weeding.
