## Findings
- Over ten fallow seasons at Akron, Colorado, the 14-month fallow precipitation storage efficiency averaged 20% under conventional tillage (range 8-34%) and 35% under no-till (range 20-51%).
- The largest difference between tillage systems was in the autumn-winter-spring period, where storage efficiency was 32% under conventional tillage and 81% under no-till.
- Fallow soil water increased by an average of 111 mm under conventional tillage and 188 mm under no-till.
- Published Great Plains wheat-fallow storage efficiencies range widely from 10% to 53%, so a single value should not be assumed for a new site.
- Storage efficiency was related to tillage, precipitation, air temperature, vapour pressure deficit and wind speed, and a simple regression on tillage and the share of small and large rainfall events can estimate it.
## Conditions and limits
- One Colorado site over ten seasons; the no-till advantage comes from reduced evaporation and better capture of small rain events, which depends on residue cover.
- Storage efficiency applies to the fallow period itself, not to the whole rotation, and a low efficiency means most fallow precipitation is lost.
- The abstract notes the measured no-till values are much lower than earlier published values for the same site, so older extension figures may be optimistic.
