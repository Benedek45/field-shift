## Findings
- In a corn-soybean rotation at Nashua, Iowa, corn grain yield and aboveground biomass did not differ significantly among no-tillage, strip-tillage and chisel plow at any nitrogen rate or nitrogen source.
- With liquid swine manure, the 3-year average maximum nitrogen rate was 182 kg N per ha and the economic optimum rate 174 kg N per ha, both giving 11.7 Mg per ha of grain.
- With commercial fertilizer nitrogen, the maximum rate was 176 kg N per ha and the economic optimum 144 kg N per ha, giving 11.2 and 11.1 Mg per ha of grain.
- Tillage and nitrogen rate interacted only in a few cases, so the nitrogen rate decision did not depend on the tillage system in this trial.
## Conditions and limits
- Three years on one Iowa soil in a corn-soybean rotation, with corn following soybean every year, so the rates are for corn after soybean, not corn after corn.
- Manure nitrogen availability depends on application timing, incorporation and weather, so the manure optimum is site-specific.
- The trial compares nitrogen sources at equal total nitrogen; it does not test whether the rotation reduces the optimal rate.
## Quotes
- "The objective of this study was to evaluate corn response to three tillage systems (no‐tillage, strip‐tillage, and chisel plow) and four N rates (0, 85, 170, and 250 kg N ha −1 ) of liquid swine manure and commercial fertilizer in a corn–soybean [ Glycine max (L.) Merr.] rotation."
- "The 3‐yr average of maximum (MNR) and economic optimal (EONR) N rates (182 and 174 kg N ha −1 , respectively) across all tillage systems with liquid manure produced identical maximum (MGY) and economic optimum (EOGY) grain yields of 11.7 Mg ha −1 ."
- "In contrast, the MNR and EONR (176 and 144 kg N ha −1 , respectively) with commercial fertilizer N produced a MGY and an EOGY of 11.2 and 11.1 Mg ha −1 , respectively."
