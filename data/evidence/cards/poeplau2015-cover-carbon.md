## Findings
- Across 139 plots at 37 sites, cover crop treatments had a significantly higher soil organic carbon stock than the reference cropland.
- The annual soil organic carbon change rate was 0.32 plus or minus 0.08 Mg per hectare per year in a mean soil depth of 22 cm, over observation periods of up to 54 years.
- The modelled steady state was reached after 155 years of cover cropping, with a total mean soil organic carbon accumulation of 16.7 plus or minus 1.5 Mg per hectare for a 22 cm soil depth.
- The authors estimated a potential global sequestration of 0.12 plus or minus 0.03 Pg C per year, equal to 8% of direct annual greenhouse gas emissions from agriculture.
## Conditions and limits
- The fit between time since cover crop introduction and soil carbon change is weak (R2 = 0.19), so the annual rate is an average, not a prediction for one field.
- Sampling depth averages 22 cm; changes below that depth are not covered, and deep sampling would change the stock estimates.
- Nitrous oxide emissions and albedo changes were not included, so this is a carbon-only budget.
- The steady state after 155 years is a model projection (RothC) under an assumed linear accumulation that cannot continue indefinitely.
