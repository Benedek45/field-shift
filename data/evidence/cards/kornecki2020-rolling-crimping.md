## Findings
- One week after rolling at the rye milk stage, rye kill was 96% with three passes of a four-stage roller, 92% with three passes of a two-stage roller and 81% with a spiral roller, against 94% for a single pass of a smooth roller with glyphosate and 37% for untreated rye.
- Two weeks after rolling there were no differences among rollers (91% to 98% kill), while untreated rye reached 54%; at three weeks all rollers reached 99% to 100% and untreated rye 86%.
- Rolling two or three times instead of once did not cause soil compaction, although cone index at 15 cm exceeded 2 MPa, which was attributed to lower gravimetric soil moisture rather than to the rolling.
- Over three growing seasons, seed cotton yield was not affected by rolling treatment, averaging 3601 kg per ha of seed cotton (1512 kg per ha of lint).
- In the southern USA the cash crop is typically planted three weeks after a rolled cover crop reaches more than 90% kill, so that it does not compete for water and nutrients.
## Conditions and limits
- Mechanical termination without herbicide needs multiple passes and still trails a herbicide-assisted single pass at one week; the 90% kill threshold is a scheduling rule, not a guarantee.
- Central Alabama humid climate with cereal rye at the milk stage; other species and growth stages respond differently to crimping.
- Yield was unaffected by termination method, so the gain from rolling is in herbicide reduction and residue management, not in yield.
## Quotes
- "At one week after rolling, the highest rye kill rate was obtained with rolling three times by the 4-stage (96%), 2-stage (92%), spiral roller (81%); rolling once by the smooth roller with glyphosate (94%) compared to the untreated rye at 37%."
