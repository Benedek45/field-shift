## Findings
- A cereal rye winter cover crop reduced flow-weighted nitrate concentration in subsurface tile drainage by 59% and nitrate load by 61%, averaged over 2002 through 2005, in a no-till corn-soybean system.
- Rye did not significantly reduce cumulative annual subsurface drainage volume, so the load reduction came from lower nitrate concentration rather than less water leaving the field.
- Eastern gamagrass grown in permanent 3.05 m wide strips above the tile lines did not significantly reduce cumulative drainage, average annual flow-weighted nitrate concentration, or cumulative nitrate load over the four comparison years.
- The experiment used twelve subsurface-drained plots of 30.5 x 42.7 m with four replicates; treatments began in 2000 and reached full establishment in autumn 2001 because of dry conditions.
## Conditions and limits
- One site only, central Iowa, no-till corn-soybean; the numbers are for subsurface drainage, not surface runoff, and do not cover conventional tillage or drier climates.
- The rye was drilled over the whole plot near harvest and chemically killed before planting the next spring; a rye cover killed late, or a cover crop that fails to establish, would not deliver the same reduction.
- Load reduction scales with drainage volume: in a year with little tile flow the absolute nitrate load saved is small even at the same percentage reduction.
