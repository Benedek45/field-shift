## Findings
- Meta-analysis of 672 observations from 63 studies in temperate climates published between 2015 and 2021: legume cover crops raised the following crop yield significantly more than grass cover crops, by 14%.
- Legume cover crops also beat non-legume broadleaves by 7% and cover crop mixtures by 2% in the following crop yield.
- Incorporating cover crop residue into the soil raised the following crop yield by about 15% compared with leaving the residue on the surface.
- In non-organic vegetable systems, grass cover crops raised yield by 14% and legume cover crops by 19% relative to no cover crop.
- Legume cover crops raised yield by 18% on coarse-textured soil and by 11% where precipitation exceeded 700 mm, while the gain on medium-textured soil was 4%.
- Cover crops increased vegetable yields and decreased silage corn yield, while grain corn, soybean and winter wheat yields did not decrease with a cover crop.
## Conditions and limits
- Pooled across temperate regions and cover crop species; the 14% legume advantage is an average over many site-years, not a promise for one field.
- The studies are recent (2015-2021) and biased toward research stations; commercial field results, such as the satellite estimate for the Corn Belt, can be less favourable.
- The residue-incorporation benefit implies tillage, which conflicts with no-till goals, so the two cannot both be taken.
