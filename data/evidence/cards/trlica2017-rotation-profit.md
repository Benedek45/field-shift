## Findings
- Over 1970-2014 on the same site, continuous corn was grown from 1970 to 1990 and corn-soybean from 1991 to 2014, with four tillage systems and five fertilizer treatments reconstructed into annual partial budgets at historical prices.
- Nitrogen fertilizer returned 396% on investment in continuous corn and 133% in the corn-soybean rotation.
- Phosphorus and potassium fertilizer returned 78% on investment in continuous corn and 109% in corn-soybean.
- Combined herbicide, machinery, labour and diesel costs were 26.6% of total costs under moldboard plow, 26.0% under chisel tillage, 21.5% under alternate tillage and 18.2% under no-till.
- In the corn-soybean phase, no-till would have been less profitable than moldboard plow only if herbicide costs rose 850%; moldboard plow would have been less profitable than no-till if machinery costs rose 300%.
## Conditions and limits
- This paper is an economic reconstruction of tillage and fertilizer treatments; it reports no corn-after-corn versus corn-after-soybean yield penalty, so it should not be cited for a rotation yield effect.
- Prices are historical and the budget omits land cost and some overheads, so the returns are partial budgets rather than whole-farm profit.
- One southern Illinois site on somewhat poorly drained soil; the continuous corn and corn-soybean phases are different decades, so tillage and rotation effects are confounded with time.
