## Findings
- In a wheat-sorghum-fallow rotation at Hays, Kansas the optimum broadcast nitrogen rate was about 60 lb N per acre applied to each crop, with the exact rate depending on soil moisture, fertilizer price and crop price.
- Soil moisture at or near planting had a larger effect on wheat and sorghum grain yield than applied nitrogen did; yield response to nitrogen was greater in years with more soil water at planting.
- Reduced tillage performed as well as or better than clean tillage and no-till in most comparisons, and left enough residue to meet conservation compliance requirements.
- No-till gave generally lower yields than the other tillage systems because of poorer stands, more weed competition and drier soil.
- After 20 years of nitrogen application up to 60 lb N per acre, most residual nitrate was in the top 6 inches of soil and there was little accumulation below that depth, so these rates were not in excess of crop needs.
## Conditions and limits
- Hays, Kansas is a semiarid silt loam site; the recommended rate reflects that rainfall and would be too low in a wetter, higher-yielding environment.
- Nitrogen was broadcast as ammonium nitrate before planting each crop; placement method and source can change the optimum rate.
- The no-till disadvantage here is a stand and weed-control result for this soil and era, not a general rule against no-till.
