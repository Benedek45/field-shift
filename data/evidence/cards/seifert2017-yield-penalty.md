## Findings
- Analysis of 748,374 joint field-level observations of yield, crop history, soil and weather across the US Midwest found an average continuous corn yield penalty of 4.3% on rainfed fields over 2007 to 2012.
- The continuous soybean yield penalty on rainfed fields averaged 10.3% over the same period.
- The continuous corn penalty was greater in locations with low moisture, and irrigation decreased the continuous corn penalty but not the continuous soybean penalty.
- Both penalties increased with the number of years of continuous cropping, but the continuous corn penalty levelled off after 3 years in corn while the continuous soybean penalty kept increasing out to a rare 5-year continuous soybean sequence.
- The continuous soybean penalty was worse in low-yielding years, while the corn penalty was more severe in areas with low moisture and low yields.
- Timely planting, favourable soil-climate and warm early and late-season minimum temperatures were associated with smaller continuous corn penalties.
## Conditions and limits
- Observational data from commercial fields, so management, variety and field quality differences between rotated and continuous fields are controlled statistically, not experimentally.
- Years 2007-2012 include the 2012 drought, which shapes the moisture and low-yield findings.
- Penalties are averages over hundreds of thousands of fields; a single field with good management can be well above or below them.
- Rainfed results are the headline; irrigated fields have a smaller continuous corn penalty.
