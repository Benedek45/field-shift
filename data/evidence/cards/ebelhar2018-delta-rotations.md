## Findings
- Cotton following corn out-yielded continuous cotton by an average of 17.1% (139.2 lb lint per acre per year) over twelve years at the Delta Research and Extension Center, with a range from -14.9% to +65.4% and negative effects in two of twelve years.
- At the satellite farm near Tribbett the rotation effect was smaller, averaging 8.9% (90.2 lb lint per acre per year) over fourteen years, with a range from -5.1% to +50.1%.
- In the Centennial Rotation the rotation effect averaged 22.0% (222 lb lint per acre per year) over the first twelve years with no negative years, and the range was +8.8% to +39.5%.
- In 2013 continuous cotton yielded 1452 lb per acre while cotton following corn yielded 1952 lb per acre, a difference of 500 lb per acre.
- Over twelve years a corn-soybean rotation removed 225% more nitrogen, 151% more phosphorus, 78% more potassium and 82% more sulfur than continuous cotton, so rotations with grain crops export more nutrients.
## Conditions and limits
- Humid Mississippi Delta with irrigation available; the rotation benefit is largest on the lighter Bosket very fine sandy loam and smaller on the silty clay loam, which holds more water.
- The negative years followed very wet growing seasons (2004 had more than 29 inches of rain from April to September), when large plants after corn suffered boll rot.
- The rotation effect compares cotton after corn with continuous cotton on the same soil; it is a lint yield effect and does not include the cost of the corn crop.
- Conference proceedings, not a peer-reviewed journal article; the trial design is sound but the statistical detail is limited.
