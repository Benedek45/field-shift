## Findings
- In a no-till wheat-sorghum-fallow-wheat sequence in the semiarid central Great Plains, there was no detectable nitrate leaching over the 4-year sequence.
- At the end of the 4-year sequence, 90% of the applied fertilizer nitrogen was accounted for at 56 kg N per ha and 87% at 112 kg N per ha.
- Of the recovered nitrogen, generally 24% to 28% remained in the soil, and crop residue deposition, immobilisation and mineralisation kept fertilizer nitrogen in the top 60 cm.
- The 10% to 13% of applied nitrogen that was unaccounted for was probably lost by denitrification or ammonia volatilisation rather than leaching.
- Both nitrogen rates raised nitrogen concentration and uptake of the first wheat crop and of the sorghum crop, but only the higher rate raised nitrogen uptake of the unfertilised second wheat crop.
## Conditions and limits
- Semiarid no-till system with a long fallow; the absence of leaching is specific to a water-limited environment, and leaching dominates where rainfall is higher.
- A nitrogen-15 microplot study, so the nitrogen balance is precise but the plots are small.
- Rates tested were 0, 56 and 112 kg N per ha, lower than typical Corn Belt corn rates.
