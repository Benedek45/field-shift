## Findings
- Tractor trips across the field were reduced by 40% in no-till cotton relative to standard tillage at Five Points, California.
- Residue cover after planting was 93% for no-till with a cover crop, 83% for no-till without, and 2% for the standard tillage systems.
- Average lint yield over four years was the same for standard tillage and no-till (1481.4 against 1484.7 kg per ha).
- Averaged over the four years, cotton without a cover crop yielded more than cotton with a cover crop (1526.1 against 1440.0 kg per ha), so the winter cover reduced lint yield in this trial.
- The authors note that about 45% of US cotton is produced with no-tillage or strip-tillage but the practice was not yet used commercially in California when the study was set up.
## Conditions and limits
- Irrigated San Joaquin Valley site with a winter cover crop in a Mediterranean climate, where cover crop growth depends on winter rain and irrigation water is scarce.
- No-till matched standard tillage for yield but did not beat it; the gain is in tractor trips, residue and dust, not in lint yield.
- The cover crop yield penalty is an average over four seasons and one cover crop mix; it does not say which species or termination date would avoid it.
