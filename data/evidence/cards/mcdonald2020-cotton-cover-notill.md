## Findings
- Over a three-year transition from conventional tillage to no-till on the Southern High Plains, cotton lint yield differed between tillage systems in each year, and no-till with a wheat cover crop out-yielded conventional tillage in the second and third years.
- Cotton seed nitrogen concentration did not differ within years, except for a decrease in the zero-nitrogen control in the third year.
- Cotton fibre strength was higher in no-till with a wheat cover crop than under conventional tillage in the second year, but conventional tillage gave higher fibre strength in 2018, attributed to late-season weather.
- No-tillage has been used on the Southern High Plains for decades mainly to reduce wind erosion, which is the practical reason to adopt it there.
## Conditions and limits
- Three-year transition period only, so the results describe the early phase, when residue and soil structure are still developing.
- The cover crop is winter wheat, not rye; termination date and irrigation are not detailed in the abstract.
- Fibre quality responses were inconsistent between years, so no quality claim should be generalised from this trial.
