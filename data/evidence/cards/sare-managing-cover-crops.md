## Findings
- Legume cover crops can contribute 15 to 200 lb of nitrogen per acre to the following cash crop, with typical values of 50 to 100 lb per acre.
- Delaying the kill date of crimson clover two weeks beyond 50% bloom and hairy vetch two weeks beyond 25% bloom increased clover biomass by 41% and vetch biomass by 61% in North Carolina, with nitrogen content up 23% for clover and 41% for vetch.
- Cereal rye is winter-hardy, grows longer into autumn and resumes growth earlier in spring than most cover crops, and produces large biomass that leaves long-lasting residue in conservation tillage.
- Cereal rye is a non-host for root-knot nematodes and several soil-borne diseases and suppresses broadleaf weeds allelopathically, including in the crops that follow it (cotton, soybean and most vegetables).
- Rye will not control weedy grasses, and in no-till it can increase cutworms and wireworms, so it is not the best cover ahead of grass crops such as corn, sorghum or pearl millet where those pests are a problem.
## Conditions and limits
- A practical handbook synthesising many trials, not a single experiment: the nitrogen credit range spans climates and legume species, and the actual credit depends on biomass and legume percentage.
- The nitrogen credit must be discounted for the legume's own establishment and for the timing of release, which depends on termination date and tillage.
- The pest cautions are regional and management-specific; they argue for species choice and termination planning rather than against cover crops in general.
