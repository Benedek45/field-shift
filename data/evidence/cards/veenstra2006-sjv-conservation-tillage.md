## Findings
- After four years of a cotton-tomato rotation on the west side of the San Joaquin Valley, conservation tillage reduced bulk density and raised available nitrate and phosphorus concentrations.
- The same conservation tillage system redistributed potassium to the soil surface, lost organic matter and increased salt concentrations, all of which the authors describe as potentially detrimental to plant growth.
- Cover cropping increased soil organic matter regardless of tillage treatment and increased potassium concentrations.
- Conservation tillage is defined here as any system leaving 30% or more of the soil surface covered with crop residue after planting.
## Conditions and limits
- Four years is short for soil organic matter and salinity trends, and the authors say the benefits of conservation tillage to soil quality are fewer and will need more research.
- The site is the saline west side of the San Joaquin Valley, where salt accumulation is a specific risk; on better-drained Valley soils the salinity result may not repeat.
- Tillage and cover crop effects are reported separately, so the combination of both is not a single tested treatment in the passage read here.
