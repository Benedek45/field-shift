## Findings
- In the Centennial Rotation, corn yield after soybean was 207 bushels per acre against 201 bushels per acre after cotton in one-year crop histories, which the author attributes largely to nitrogen fixed by soybean.
- Corn averaged 198 bushels per acre after a two-year soybean-corn history and 206 bushels per acre in cotton-cotton-corn and soybean-corn-soybean three-year histories.
- Soybean mean yield was 60 bushels per acre after cotton and 59 bushels per acre after corn in one-year histories, and reached 61 bushels per acre in a cotton-corn two-year history.
- Treating rotations as portfolios of crops, the efficient mix contained 0% cotton with about 67% corn and 33% soybeans, so cotton did not enter the risk-efficient combination in this analysis.
- Corn weights in the analysed portfolios ranged from 54% to 65% and soybean weights from 35% to 46%.
## Conditions and limits
- Economic analysis of one long-term rotation experiment at Stoneville, Mississippi; the portfolio result depends on the price series used and on the historical yields of that site.
- The report is an extension and promotion-board deliverable, not a peer-reviewed paper, and the analysis reports means without full statistical tests.
- Cotton was excluded from the efficient portfolios partly because of the low cotton prices in the study period, not because cotton cannot be profitable in the Delta.
