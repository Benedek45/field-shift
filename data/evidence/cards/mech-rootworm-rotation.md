## Findings
- Rotating maize with another crop, principally soybean, was the primary management strategy for western corn rootworm in North America and stayed highly effective until the mid-1990s.
- In 1995 widespread severe root injury occurred in east-central Illinois and northern Indiana maize fields that had been annually rotated with soybean for several decades.
- The failure was attributed to a behavioural adaptation: a variant western corn rootworm that had lost fidelity to maize for egg laying, so eggs were laid in the rotated crop and larvae survived into the next maize year.
- By 2007 the insect had been confirmed in 20 European countries, and at least three separate invasions of Europe had been detected up to 2004.
- Biological control and conventional resistant hybrids had not achieved widespread success against western corn rootworm in North America at the time of the review.
## Conditions and limits
- Rotation still works against the widespread, maize-faithful rootworm population; the failure described is a regional variant in parts of Illinois and Indiana, and the review does not measure yield loss quantitatively.
- Continuous maize with a soil insecticide or Bt hybrid is the alternative considered, and both bring resistance-management problems of their own.
- A review of pest biology, not a rotation trial: use it for the mechanism and the geography, not for a yield coefficient.
