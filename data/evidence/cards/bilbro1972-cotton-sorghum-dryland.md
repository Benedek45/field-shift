## Findings
- In a 36-year dryland study at Lubbock, Texas, the mean lint yield of continuous cotton was 19 kg per ha greater than cotton grown in rotation with grain sorghum, and the difference was not statistically significant.
- Dryland cotton yields should exceed 150, 250, 400 and 600 kg per ha in about 30%, 50%, 25% and 10% of years respectively, so the rotation does not change the risk profile of cotton much.
- Grain sorghum grown in rotation with cotton had a significantly higher mean yield than continuous grain sorghum.
- Dryland continuous grain sorghum should exceed 750, 1200, 2000 and 2750 kg per ha in about 70%, 50%, 25% and 10% of years respectively, with slightly higher yields when sorghum alternates with cotton.
- Substituting hybrids for open-pollinated varieties was expected to raise dryland yields by 10% to 20%.
## Conditions and limits
- Data are from 1914-1949, before modern varieties, fertiliser and herbicides, so absolute yields are far below current levels; the risk comparison is what transfers.
- Dryland only: on irrigated ground the rotation effects on cotton differ, and cotton-sorghum rotations are usually judged on water and pest effects rather than yield alone.
- Cotton showed no rotation yield benefit here; the benefit in this experiment went to sorghum.
