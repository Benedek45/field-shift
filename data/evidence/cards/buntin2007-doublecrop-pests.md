## Findings
- In twelve double-cropping sequences at Plains, Georgia, Hessian fly infestation of wheat was lower after canola than after wheat, but tended to be higher after pearl millet than after soybean.
- Sclerotinia stem rot incidence in canola was greater where canola was grown continuously.
- Stands of both pearl millet and soybean were reduced after canola compared with after wheat, and false chinch bug infestations were greater on seedling stands of both summer crops after canola.
- Both summer crops compensated for stand differences, so grain yield was not affected by the previous winter crop.
- Soybean stem canker infection was not affected by the previous crop sequence, and pearl millet generally had little effect on pest incidence or agronomic performance of the other crops.
## Conditions and limits
- Southeastern double-crop system with canola as a new winter crop; the pest effects are specific to that region and to the insects and diseases named.
- Stand reductions after canola are a real risk that yield compensation only partly offsets, and the authors say they would need to be mitigated before canola could be used.
- Companion paper to a take-all rotation study at the same site, so it shares the same experimental design and region.
