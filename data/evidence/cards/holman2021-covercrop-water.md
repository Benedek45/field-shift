## Findings
- Twelve short-season cover crops were tested in a winter wheat-fallow rotation at Garden City, Kansas; available soil water at cover crop planting was greater for spring-planted than for autumn or winter cover crops.
- Water use, cover crop productivity and post-cover-crop fallow efficiency were greatest for sole winter triticale and for winter legume-triticale mixtures.
- Available soil water at winter wheat planting was 4% to 27% greater when the cover crop was left standing than when it was hayed.
- Post-cover-crop precipitation storage was positively related to available soil water at wheat planting, while cover crop biomass had a negative linear relationship with it, so bulky covers cost water.
## Conditions and limits
- Semiarid Kansas site with about 15 months of fallow in the wheat-fallow system; the trade-off is between fallow water storage and cover crop benefits.
- Leaving the cover crop standing conserved more water than haying it, but haying gives forage income, which the paper treats as a dual-purpose option rather than a loss.
- The cover crops were short-season species sown inside the fallow period, not full-season covers before a summer crop.
