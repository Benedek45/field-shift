## Findings
- Conservation tillage cut tractor trips across the field by 50% for tomatoes and 40% for cotton compared with standard tillage at Five Points, California.
- Averaged over 2001 to 2003, tomato yields in the conservation tillage system without a cover crop were 6 to 8 tons per acre higher than the other treatments.
- In cotton, the standard tillage system without a cover crop gave the highest yields over 2001 to 2003 and was 276 pounds per acre higher than conservation tillage without a cover crop.
- In-field dust concentrations were significantly reduced by conservation tillage.
- The authors conclude conservation tillage may be a viable alternative for tomato and cotton in the San Joaquin Valley but that fine-tuning of the systems is needed.
## Conditions and limits
- Transition period only (1999-2003): conservation tillage typically performs poorly while soil structure and residue levels adjust, so these yields understate the long-term case.
- Results differ by crop: tomato gained from conservation tillage, cotton lost yield, so the rotation effect depends on which crop is in which system.
- Irrigated San Joaquin Valley site; furrow and drip irrigation infrastructure affects how well residue is retained and how the beds are rebuilt.
